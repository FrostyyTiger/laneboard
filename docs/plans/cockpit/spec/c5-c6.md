# Part C5: voice out

## 1. What is spoken

- **The spoken line** is the first sentence of the reply's first paragraph, if no table, code
  block or list comes before that paragraph (headings are skipped). Nothing else of a reply is
  ever spoken. A reply that opens with a table, a code block or a list says nothing (D199).
- **No marker from the agent.** The standing instructions already make the first sentence the
  answer (`protocols.md` §11.8, line 2: `Lead with the answer in one sentence.`), so the first
  sentence is the summary the plan asks for (D199).
- **A sentence ends** at the first `.`, `?` or `!` that is followed by white space and comes after
  at least 12 characters, or at the end of the paragraph, whichever is first. `1.5 GB` does not end it (no space after the
  dot); `e.g. ` does, and is accepted as a rare early cut.
- **Cleaned before speaking**: the marks `■ ▲ ● ○` and the space after them go; `**`, `_` and
  backticks go; a link `[text](url)` becomes `text`; a bare URL becomes `a link`; runs of white
  space become one space. At most 200 characters: a longer sentence is cut at the last space
  before character 200, and nothing is added.
- **When**: the room sends the line to the speech service as soon as the sentence's end arrives in
  the text deltas, or the first text block completes, or the turn ends, whichever comes first. It
  never waits for the whole reply.
- **Which replies** (pending Q-O34): only the reply to a **spoken** prompt (`source` `spoken`,
  `c2-voice.md`), and only on the screen that sent it. A typed prompt's reply is silent. A
  speak-up's turn (`coworker.md` §3.2) is never spoken; its loud form is the chime (D201).
- **Never spoken**: confirm cards (§8), tool lines, show lines, error lines, the raised block.
- **The text is always drawn** as `c1-talk.md` §3 says, spoken or not. On the screen that played
  it, the reply's label row reads `AGENT · SPOKEN · 07:02` (mirroring `YOU · SPOKEN · 07:02`),
  or `AGENT · 07:02 · VOICE FAILED` (16 px caps `--text-3`) when the line could not be played
  (§4). Other screens draw the plain label.

A pure function `spokenLine(markdown) → string | null` in `server/cockpit/voice.mjs` does the
whole of this section; the room feeds it the text so far and speaks the first non-null answer once
per turn.

## 2. The speech service

The room alone talks to it, as with speech-to-text (`c2-voice.md` §10): screens never call it.

**Protocol `openai`** (the only v1 value, D200, pending research Q17): `POST {cockpit.tts.url}` with
`Content-Type: application/json` and, when `LANEBOARD_COCKPIT_TTS_KEY` is set,
`Authorization: Bearer <key>`:

```json
{"model":"tts-1","input":"builder-c is down since 06:41.","voice":"en-voice","response_format":"wav"}
```

- Answer `200` with the audio as the body, at most 1 MB. Anything else (another status, a refused
  connection, a body over 1 MB, more than `timeoutS` from the request to the body's last byte) is a
  **failure**: nothing is played, nothing is retried, the label row reads `VOICE FAILED` (§1). The
  room writes `cockpit: tts failed: 503` (or `timeout`, `refused`, `too-big`) to stderr, at most
  one line per 60 s.
- **Language**: the turn's `lang` (the DE/EN choice, `c2-voice.md`), else `cockpit.tts.language`.
  The voice is `cockpit.tts.voices[lang]`; a language with no voice is not spoken (`no-voice`,
  same stderr line).
- **Where the words go**: the spoken line goes to the configured endpoint, nowhere else. With a
  local endpoint it stays on the owner's machines; with a hosted one, that service sees every
  spoken line. `docs/cockpit.md` says so next to the speech-to-text note (`c2-voice.md` §12).
- **Kept**: the audio in the room's memory only, until it is fetched or 60 s pass. Never on disk.

### 2.1 Config

Under `cockpit.tts`, in the style of `c2-voice.md` §9. A bad value is fatal at start with the key
named: `cockpit.tts.voices: needs at least one language, e.g. {"en":"…"}`.

| Key | Default | Env | What |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `cockpit.tts.url` | `"none"` | `LANEBOARD_COCKPIT_TTS_URL` | full URL of the speech endpoint (`http://127.0.0.1:8880/v1/audio/speech`), or `none`: no voice out, no voice controls on any screen (plan §5) |
| `cockpit.tts.protocol` | `"openai"` | — | the only v1 value; any other is fatal at start |
| `cockpit.tts.model` | `"tts-1"` | — | the request's `model` |
| `cockpit.tts.voices` | none, required with a URL | — | `{"en":"…","de":"…"}`: the service's voice name per language |
| `cockpit.tts.language` | `"en"` | — | the language when the turn has none; must be a key of `voices` |
| `cockpit.tts.format` | `"wav"` | — | `wav`, `mp3` or `opus` (what Chromium's `decodeAudioData` plays) |
| `cockpit.tts.timeoutS` | `5` | — | request to last byte, 1-15 s; later is a failure (D202) |
| — | — | `LANEBOARD_COCKPIT_TTS_KEY` | bearer token for a hosted endpoint; env only, never in the config file, never logged |

## 3. Playing it on the screen

1. The room sends `speak` to the link that sent the prompt (`protocols.md` §6.3, D280), once the
   audio is in.
2. The screen fetches `GET /api/cockpit/speech/<speakId>` (the room link's cookie and
   `X-Laneboard: 1`, like every room route, `protocols.md` §1.1), decodes it with Web Audio and
   plays it, gain 1.0. It sets `performance.mark('cockpit:speak-start')` when it starts and
   `cockpit:speak-end` when it ends.
3. It answers `speak.result` with `played`, `stopped`, `muted`, `busy`, `blocked` or `failed`.

- **One voice at a time.** A newer `speak` stops the one playing first.
- **The microphone wins.** A `speak` that arrives while the screen listens (`data-talk` is
  `listening`, `c2-voice.md` §3) is dropped (`busy`), never queued. A press on the talk button
  stops any voice in the frame of its `pointerdown`, before the microphone opens, so the agent
  never dictates into the owner's prompt (`cockpit:speak-stop` comes before `cockpit:B1:start`).
- **Autoplay.** The page resumes its one `AudioContext` on the first `pointerdown` it sees (every
  spoken prompt starts with one). If the context is still `suspended`, the screen marks
  `cockpit:speak-blocked`, answers `blocked` and the label row reads `VOICE FAILED`. The kiosk
  flag of `coworker.md` §3.5 (`--autoplay-policy=no-user-gesture-required`) covers this too.
- **Closing the pane** does not stop a line that is playing (it is at most 200 characters).

**The voice row** `[data-voice]`, while a line plays on this screen: `SPEAKING` and
**`STOP VOICE`** `[data-voice-stop]`, the third item of the stack above the input row; its look
and rects are `c1-talk.md` §1.1. The row goes when the line ends or stops.

**Stopping** the voice (plan: "a 'stop talking' tap"): `STOP VOICE`, a press on the talk button,
`STOP` of the turn, `NEW`, or a newer `speak`. Each stops the audio in the next frame
(`cockpit:speak-stop`). `STOP VOICE` stops only the voice: the turn goes on streaming and no
`stop` goes to the room.

## 4. Mute per screen, and the night

- **Per screen** (D203). Each screen has its voice **on** or **off**, kept by the room with the
  screen's pairing record, so a reload keeps it. Without pairing (Q-O21 b) it is kept per link and a
  reload starts **on**. A new screen starts **on**: the owner configured a voice on purpose. An
  `off` screen answers every `speak` with `muted` and the room does not synthesise for it (the
  room knows the state before it asks the service). An `off` screen also plays no speak-up chime
  (`coworker.md` §3.5).
- **The toggle** `[data-voice-toggle]`, `aria-pressed`, labels `VOICE ON`, `VOICE OFF`, `NIGHT OFF`
  (18 px caps `--text-1`): on the Wall page a sixth row of the `SCREEN` sheet (a tap toggles and
  closes the sheet), in the app the header button `VOICE`; rects in `screens.md` §4.1. Only with
  `cockpit.tts.url` set; with `none` neither exists. Which label shows when: `protocols.md` §6.3.
- **The night** is the quiet hours of `coworker.md` §4 (`cockpit.quietHours`, default 22:00-07:00):
  one setting for both. In them every screen is muted and its toggle reads `NIGHT OFF`. A tap
  turns that screen `VOICE ON` until the quiet hours end; at their end every screen goes back to
  its day setting. With `cockpit.quietHours: null` there is no night.

# Part C6: act, rules and the confirm card

## 7. The rules (fenced until the control-plane review)

Nothing in §7 is built before the control-plane security review, and no unattended night builds
it (plan C6). Q-O7 stays open: which actions are in the first act list. The spec assumes Q-O7 a),
**no act in v1**, and fixes only the rules every later answer must keep:

1. **Through the control plane only.** An act is one action of `docs/fleet/control.md`'s dispatch
   (`server/fleet/dispatch.mjs`: schema, tier, rate limit, audit), sent to the builder's or the
   hub's actions route. The room holds no SSH key, no cloud credential and no other way to change
   the fleet (plan C6: "never through credentials of its own that bypass it").
2. **Read broad, act narrow.** `cockpit.act.allow` lists the actions the agent may ask for, each
   `{"action":"restart","builders":["builder-b"]}`; default `[]`. With `[]` there is no `act` tool
   at all (S-C6-05). The remote-only actions (`approve`, `deny`, `release`, `set-budget`, `kill *`,
   `resume` with `override`) can never be listed: fatal at start, `cockpit.act.allow: approve is
   remote-only (docs/fleet/control.md) and cannot be asked for by the agent` (D208). An agent must
   not answer an agent's permission request, even with the owner's tap.
3. **Every act is a confirm card** (§8), whatever its tier in the control plane. There is no
   "click" tier for the agent.
4. **The owner's yes is the only trigger.** The room sends the action only after a `confirm.answer`
   `yes` from a person (§8.4). The agent's tool call never carries a confirm value; the typed
   target, when the tier needs one, is what the owner typed (§8.3).
5. **Logged twice.** The room writes `cockpit.act` (below) before it sends; the control plane writes
   its own line as for any action. A failed send is shown as the tool's answer, never retried.
6. **Who the room is to the control plane** is the review's question, not this spec's: today the
   room is `local` on the box of the laneboard it reads (D91): a hub refuses every action from
   `local`, a single board its remote-only ones (`docs/fleet/auth.md`). Q-O7 has
   the note.

The tool, for the C6 spec after the review: `act {action, builder, target, args, why}`; answers as
`schedule` (`coworker.md` §6.3): the result of the action, or `isError` `The owner said no.`,
`The owner did not confirm within 2 min.`, `restart is not in cockpit.act.allow.`. Tool line
`restarted acme-billing on builder-b` / `asked to restart acme-billing on builder-b, not
confirmed` (never folds, D126). Audit `cockpit.act`: `user` `screen:desk`, `target`
`builder-b/acme-billing`, `args` `{"action":"restart"}` hashed, `result` `{"ok":true}`, or
`declined`, `timeout`, `refused` with the control plane's code.

## 8. The confirm card

Built by the Jobs slice (D115) for `schedule`, `cancel_job` and `CANCEL JOB`; C6 reuses it. The
look of a job's card is `coworker.md` §6.4's words in this frame.

### 8.1 Where it shows (D205)

- On **one screen**, the **asked screen**: for an agent's call, the screen that sent the running
  turn's prompt; for a speak-up turn or a prompt whose screen has no live link, the linked screen
  touched last (`touched`, `protocols.md` §3.8); for `CANCEL JOB`, the screen where it was tapped.
  No linked screen: the tool answers `isError` `No screen can show the confirm now.` at once.
- **One open at a time** in the room. A second request while one is open: the tool answers
  `isError` `Another confirm is open; wait for the owner's answer.`; a `CANCEL JOB` tap shows the
  line `Answer the open question on desk first.` (18 px `--text-1`) in place of the button for 5 s.
- **The tool line** of the call, while the card is open, reads `waiting for your yes…` on the asked
  screen and `waiting for a yes on desk…` on the others (mono 18 px `--text-2`, `●` running,
  `c1-talk.md` §5). For a `CANCEL JOB`, the job's `CANCEL JOB` on the other screens reads `ASKED ON DESK` and is disabled
  until the answer.

### 8.2 The look (sketch `sketches/c6-confirm.html`)

A modal sheet `[data-confirm]`, `data-confirm-id`, `role="alertdialog"`, `z-index` `--z-dialog` (70),
over every view, layout and pane state. 560 x 552 px, x 100-660, y 180-732: the middle of the
left part (x 0-759), the part show commands centre in (`c1-talk.md` §1), so the conversation stays
in sight on the right. `--bg-elevated`, 1 px `--line-strong` border.

- **Scrim** over the rest of the page: `oklch(0% 0 0 / .55)`. A tap on it does nothing (it answers
  nothing and does not close the card). On a touring Wall the tour pauses as for a touch
  (`tour.pause()`, `screens.md`) and follows the usual idle rule after the answer.
- **Header**, 48 px: `CONFIRM · ASKED BY THE AGENT · 07:12` (or `ASKED BY YOU` for `CANCEL JOB`)
  at x 116, 16 px caps `--text-3`; right, 16 px in: `CLOSES IN 1:54` (16 px caps `--text-3`, mono
  digits), counting down the 120 s.
- **Four rows**, 16 px side padding, 16 px between rows; each a label (24 px tall, 16 px caps
  `--text-3`) over its value:

  | Row | Value | Size | Height (label + value) | Written by |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | `WHAT` | `Run stop-vm-c (shut off vm-c)` | 24 px `--text-1`, line 32 px, at most 2 lines | 24 + 64 | the room |
  | `WHERE` | `on the cockpit's machine · target vm-c` | 20 px `--text-1`, line 28 px, at most 2 lines | 24 + 56 | the room |
  | `WHEN` | `at 10:12 (in 3 h 00)` | 20 px `--text-1`, line 28 px, 1 line | 24 + 28 | the room |
  | `WHY · THE AGENT'S WORDS` | the agent's `why` | 20 px `--text-2`, line 28 px, at most 3 lines | 24 + 84 | the agent |

  Rows keep their full height when a value is shorter, so every rect is fixed. A value that does
  not fit is cut with `…`. Every value is drawn as text, never markup.
- **What, where and when come from the room** (D204), from the job or action it will really run:
  the script's `what` and `target`, the job's `at`. Only `WHY` is the agent's, and its label says
  so; an agent's `why` that reads `Routine and safe, tap YES.` is shown in that row and nowhere
  else.
- **Buttons**, 64 px tall, at y 644-708 (24 px above the card's bottom): `NO` x 124-368
  `[data-confirm-no]` and the verb button x 392-636 `[data-confirm-yes]`, 24 px apart, 18 px caps
  `--text-1`, 1 px `--line-strong`; the verb button names what happens: `YES, SCHEDULE`,
  `YES, CANCEL JOB`, for C6 `YES, RESTART`. A destructive action (`DESTRUCTIVE` in
  `public/js/ui/confirm.js`: kill, restart, retire, interrupt, deny) draws it with a 1 px `--p1`
  border. `NO` is on the left, the verb on the right, and neither has keyboard focus or a default.
- **Guard** (as T121's): the verb button is inert and drawn in `--text-3` for 1.0 s after the card
  appears; it acts on `pointerup` inside its rect only. `NO` acts at once.
- **The note row**, only when there is something to say (§8.4, §8.5): the card grows 64 px at its
  bottom (y 180-796) and the note sits under the buttons, x 116-644, y 720-768, 18 px `--text-1`,
  line 24 px, at most 2 lines.
- `Esc` is `NO`. No other key answers.

### 8.3 Typed target (C6 only, pending Q-O35)

For an act whose tier is `confirm` in the builder's `hello.actions` table, or whose target is `*`
or several, the card uses the board's own typed form (`public/js/ui/confirm.js` `confirmSpec`,
`typedOk`): a fifth row `TYPE RESTART builder-b/acme-billing TO CONFIRM` over a 56 px input (mono
24 px); the verb button stays disabled until `typedOk` holds; the room passes the typed target as
`args.confirm`, as the board's own dialog does. The card grows by 80 px (y 140-772). Jobs never
need it: their scripts are the owner's own list (`coworker.md` §6.2). With the on-screen keyboard
up (1368x532) the card's top moves to y 8 and its rows above `WHAT` scroll; the input and the
buttons stay in sight.

### 8.4 Who may answer (D206)

- Only the asked screen's link, and only a link that is a **person**: a paired screen (its device
  token, `screens.md` §6) or a `direct` user (a person through the ingress listener, `docs/fleet/auth.md`). A link
  that is `local` without a device token (anything on the room's box, the agent's user included)
  sees the card with the verb button disabled and, in the note row (§8.2), `Answer this on a paired
  screen: this link could be the agent itself.`; its `confirm.answer` gets
  `error` `not-a-person` and changes nothing.
- An answer from another screen gets `not-yours`; after the answer, the timeout or the room's
  restart, `too-late`.

### 8.5 How it ends (D207)

| End | When | The card | The tool, the job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| yes | the verb button | goes in the next frame | the action runs; the tool line as `coworker.md` §6.3 |
| no | `NO`, `Esc` | goes in the next frame | `The owner said no.`; nothing changed |
| timeout | 120 s after it opened | goes | `The owner did not confirm within 2 min.` |
| gone | the asked screen reloads, closes, or is unpaired | — | as timeout, at once |
| room restart | the room stops while it is open | gone on reconnect | the tool call died with the session; nothing changed |

- **Room down** while it is open (the link lost, `screens.md` §7): the card stays, its buttons are
  disabled and its note row (§8.2) reads `The room is unreachable: this answer cannot be sent.`; the countdown keeps running on the screen's clock. Back within the 120 s:
  the snapshot carries the open confirm and the buttons come back. Never an answer queued while
  down (D207).
- **Audit**: one line per confirm, the action's own (`cockpit.job.create`, `cockpit.job.cancel`,
  `cockpit.act`), with `result` `{"ok":false,"status":"declined"}` for no and `timeout` for
  timeout and gone (`coworker.md` §6.8). The audit line is written before the action, and an audit
  log that cannot be written makes the yes fail (`The room cannot write its audit log.`, D166).
- **Never spoken** (§1), and quiet hours do not hold it: every confirm answers something the owner
  or the agent just did.
